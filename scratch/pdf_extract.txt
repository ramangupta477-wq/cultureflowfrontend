1. Purpose, audience and scope of v1
The v1 site is a small, fast credibility-and-waitlist site: it proves Culture Flow is real and
safe, and captures signups before the first program opens on Day 45. A visitor should be
able to answer four questions within 60 seconds: who are they, what do they offer, is it
safe, and how do I join.
Goals
1. Explain what Culture Flow does in under 10 seconds on the home page.
2. Give parents verifiable trust signals: company details, safety system, policies, named
people.
3. Capture waitlist signups (students and parents) and institution enquiries.
4. Give Instagram visitors a landing page that matches the brand and loads fast on mobile.
Audiences
In scope for v1: a mobile-first marketing site, a waitlist form, an institution enquiry form, a
contact page, and legal pages.
Deferred to the full platform: online enrollment and payments, user accounts and login,
program booking, itinerary or pricing calculators, a blog CMS, and multiple languages.

4.4 How It Works
This page explains the model and the student journey, and is the one partner institutions
read most closely.
1. The 40/40/20 model. Three short blocks. 40% academic rigor and hands-on
workshops; 40% industry and cultural immersion; 20% guided synthesis and
reflection. Each block gets two or three example activities [FILL].
2. The journey. Join the waitlist, apply and get selected, attend a pre-departure briefing
(parents invited), complete the program, then reflect and receive outcomes.
3. A week at a glance. One illustrative timetable showing how the three blocks sit across
days.
4. Why short immersions. Three honest points on why 7 to 21 days works: fits inside a
semester break, lower cost and commitment than a semester abroad, focused
outcomes.
5. Call to action. Join the Waitlist; for institutions, Partner with us.

Rules for the tech team: every form carries an unticked consent checkbox linking to the
Privacy Policy; no data is collected before consent; if a visitor could be under 18, add an
age confirmation. Rules for copy: never promise a job, internship, visa or admission
outcome, and never show a testimonial or partner logo you cannot document.
6. Brand and design requirements
The site should feel clean, institutional and prestigious: navy-led, generous white space,
one gold accent for action. Use the existing brand kit exactly.
Contrast rules (checked): navy text on gold is about 6.4 to 1 and passes accessibility AA,
so gold buttons use navy text. White text on gold is about 2.5 to 1 and fails, so never use it.
Gold text works on navy, but not on white.
Typography: Oranienbaum for headings and display text. TT Norms for body and UI text.
Oranienbaum is a free Google Font. TT Norms is a paid commercial font, so the team must
confirm a web-font licence before using it; if there is none, use a free sans such as
Manrope or DM Sans as the body font. Body text 16 px minimum on mobile; headings scale
down cleanly to a 360 px wide screen.
Logo: the interlocking CF monogram with the ascending flight trail, supplied as SVG.
Provide a full-colour version, a white version for navy backgrounds, a favicon (32 and 512
px) and a social-share image (1200 by 630 px). Keep clear space equal to the height of the
"C" on all sides and never recolour or stretch it.
Imagery rules:
1. Prefer real photography from scouting visits, pilot sessions and partners, with written
permission.
2. Show structure: timetables, workshops, notebooks, industry visits, people at work.

3. No generic tourist stock photos, no AI-generated destination scenes, no fake
testimonials. These push the brand toward "holiday" and "scam".
4. Until real photos exist, use brand-colour graphics, the flight-trail motif and typographic
layouts.
Tone of voice: precise, confident, warm. Use program, cohort, immersion, syllabus,
outcomes. Avoid trip, tour, holiday, package, dream destination, once in a lifetime.
Motion: subtle only. The flight trail may draw once on the hero; respect the reduced-
motion setting and keep it under one second.
7. Technical requirements
Build v1 as a fast, mostly static site with a small form backend, hosted cheaply on a CDN,
so it loads quickly on Indian mobile networks and can be replaced or extended when the
full platform launches. The stack is the tech team's call; the requirements below are what
the result must meet.
